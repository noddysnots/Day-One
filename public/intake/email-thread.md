Standard case: invoice comes in, we match it to the PO and to the goods receipt. If the
three agree, it gets paid on the vendor's terms. No ceremony.

Tolerance. Two percent or fifty dollars, whichever is lower. That is the whole test. If
the invoice is over the PO by more than that, it does not get paid on my say-so, it comes
to you.

Low value: anything under five hundred dollars that matches the PO and the receipt, just
pay it. I am not spending your time on a box of gloves.

Adding the two that bite us most often.

Short receipts. If the invoice bills more units than the dock signed for, we do not pay
the difference. Reject it and go back to the vendor for a corrected invoice or a credit.
We have paid for air twice this year and I am not doing it again.

Duplicates. Same vendor, same amount, inside fourteen days of an invoice we already have
on file — treat it as a duplicate and reject it. Northline are the worst for this, their AR
system re-sends anything still open.

No PO on the invoice. Those come to me. Someone bought something off-process and I have to
find out who owns it before it gets paid. Do not guess the PO.

Marcus — for a stale PO, meaning one cut before the revision date, I will go up to five
percent over the PO value provided the unit prices on the invoice match the current price
list. That is the test: stale PO, under five percent, unit prices are the live ones. If all
three hold, approve it and do not send it to Priya. If the unit prices do not match the
current list, that is a different conversation and I want to see it.

This applies to the revision situation specifically. It is not a general five percent
tolerance and I do not want it read that way.

Also on tax: Vantage bill us eight percent by contract. If their tax line is not eight
percent of the subtotal, something is wrong on their side, do not just pay it.
